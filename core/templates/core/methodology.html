Methodology

{# Intro card #}

How Comparative Analytics Work

The Comparative Analytics card on your Bibliotype dashboard shows three stats that place your reading habits alongside both a global average drawn from published research and a community percentile calculated from the reading data of all Bibliotype users.

This page explains exactly what each stat measures, how it is computed from your data, where the global benchmark comes from, and what caveats apply.

{# Book Length section #}

Average Book Length

What it measures

The mean page count of all books on your read shelf that have page count data available, rounded to the nearest whole page.

How your value is computed

Bibliotype reads the page count for each book from your export and supplements missing values by querying Open Library and Google Books. Only books with a resolved page count contribute to the average — books where no source reports a page count are excluded.

Community percentile

Once your average is computed, it is ranked against the same average calculated for every other Bibliotype reader. The percentile tells you what share of readers has a lower average book length than you.

Assumptions and caveats

  • If enrichment is still in progress, some books may not yet have a page count and the figure will update automatically once enrichment completes.
  • Different editions of the same book can have very different page counts. Bibliotype uses whichever edition Open Library or Google Books returns first, which may not match the edition you read.
  • Very short books (e.g. novellas) and very long ones (e.g. omnibus editions) are included without trimming, so a single unusually long book can shift your average noticeably.

{# Book Age section #}

Average Book Age

What it measures

The mean original publication year of all books on your read shelf that have a known publish year, giving a sense of whether you tend to read newer releases or reach further back into the backlist.

How your value is computed

Bibliotype reads the publication year from your export and fills gaps via Open Library and Google Books. Only books with a resolved publication year contribute to the average.

Community percentile

Your average publish year is ranked against the same figure for every other Bibliotype reader. A higher percentile means your reading skews more recent compared to the community; a lower percentile means you tend to read older books.

Assumptions and caveats

  • If enrichment is still in progress, some books may be missing a publish year and the figure will update automatically once enrichment completes.
  • Bibliotype uses the original publication year where possible, not the year of a reprint or new edition. If Open Library or Google Books only provides an edition date, that is used instead.
  • Books from ancient or medieval periods can pull the average down significantly if you read a lot of classics.

Global benchmark: The average publish year of a book read by the average reader is {{ global_averages.avg_publish_year }}. Source: {{ global_averages_sources.avg_publish_year.title }}.

{# Books Per Year section #}

Books Per Year

What it measures

Your average annual reading pace, calculated only from books with a recorded finish date in your export.

How your value is computed

Bibliotype counts the number of books with a recorded finish date, then divides by the number of distinct calendar years in which you finished at least one book. For example, if you finished 50 books spread across 5 different years, your pace is 10 books a year.

Community percentile

Your books-per-year figure is ranked against the same figure for every other Bibliotype reader. The percentile tells you what share of readers has a lower annual reading pace than you.

Assumptions and caveats

  • Only books with a recorded finish date count. If you mark books as read on Goodreads without entering a date, they are excluded and your pace may appear lower than reality.
  • The calculation uses the number of years in which you finished at least one book, not the total span between your first and last recorded finish date. A reading gap of several years does not pull your average down.
  • If you finished only one book or all your books fall in the same calendar year, the community percentile comparison is omitted and a simpler total-books count is shown instead.

Global benchmark: The average reader reads {{ global_averages.avg_books_per_year }} books a year. Source: {{ global_averages_sources.avg_books_per_year.title }}.
